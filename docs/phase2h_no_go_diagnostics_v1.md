## Why NO_GO happened

The Phase 2G-C run cleared every *data* gate (35,014 rows, 41 tickers incl. SPY,
854 trading dates, 820 scored cross-sections) but failed the *signal* gates of
the canary harness. The composite shows no usable cross-sectional ranking edge
over 2023-02 → 2026-05:

| Metric | Value | Gate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| rank_ic (reported) | `0.00138` | ≥ `0.03` | **fail** — at noise |
| rank_ic (recomputed here) | `-0.00201` over 820 dates | ≥ `0.03` | **fail** — confirms noise |
| top_decile_hit_rate | `0.4899` | ≥ `0.50` | **fail** — below coin-flip |
| top_decile vs universe excess | `0.00183` vs `0.00011` | top > universe | marginal, not robust |
| bucket_monotonic | `false` | monotone | **fail** — quintiles not ordered |
| legacy_comparison_available | `false` | required | **fail** — no baseline wired |
| sample size | 820 dates / 40 names | ≥ 120 / ≥ 20 | pass — sample is adequate |

The recomputed rank IC (`-0.00201`, Spearman-on-ranks, no scipy) independently
reproduces the harness result: the signal is statistically indistinguishable
from zero.

### Is this a data, signal, or gate issue?

**Primarily a signal issue.** The export and feature pipeline are sound and the
sample is large enough, so this is not a data-size problem. The model — an
equal-weight cross-sectional mean-z composite — simply has no demonstrated
ranking power on this universe and horizon. A secondary **gate issue** also
applies: no legacy-prediction comparison was wired in, which the canary gate
additionally requires before any `GO_CANDIDATE`, so even a borderline-positive
signal could not have passed as-is.

### Rank IC is unstable across time, averaging to zero

Per-year mean per-date rank IC (n_dates):

| Year | rank_ic | n_dates |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | `-0.0198` | 221 |
| 2024 | `+0.0205` | 252 |
| 2025 | `-0.0344` | 250 |
| 2026 (partial) | `+0.0635` | 97 |

The sign flips year to year (and quarter to quarter: 2023-Q3 `-0.089`, 2024-Q1
`+0.092`, 2025-Q4 `-0.065`). There is no stable, persistent ranking signal; the
near-zero full-sample IC is the average of cancelling regimes, not a small-but-
real edge. The lone positive stretch is the thin partial-year 2026 window (97
dates, through May) and is not evidence of edge.

### Top decile does not lead, and buckets are not monotone

Top-decile 5d excess by year is negative in 2023 (`-0.0026`) and 2025
(`-0.0010`), positive only in 2024 (`+0.0016`) and the thin 2026 window; hit
rate is below 50% in three of four years. Score quintiles are non-monotone —
mean realized excess by bin is `+0.075%`, `-0.007%`, `-0.180%`, `+0.085%`,
`+0.084%` — so higher score does not map to higher forward return.

### Ticker-level behavior is inconsistent

Per-ticker rank IC ranges from `+0.068` (AMD) to `-0.192` (AMZN), with several
large negatives (AVGO `-0.139`, BAC `-0.121`, WMT `-0.119`). Names where the
composite ranks *backwards* roughly offset the names where it ranks correctly —
again consistent with no aggregate edge rather than a few broken tickers.

## Recommended next experiments

Research only — none of these is enabled, deployed, or traded here.

1. **Feature reweighting.** Replace the equal-weight mean-z composite with an
   out-of-sample fitted cross-sectional weighting (ridge / rank-regression on
   point-in-time features), validated walk-forward to avoid lookahead.
2. **Horizon sweep.** Re-score at 10d / 21d / 63d forward horizons; the 5d
   excess target may be dominated by noise at the weekly horizon.
3. **Feature pruning.** Drop features with near-zero univariate per-date rank IC
   and isolate the momentum vs mean-reversion blocks separately.
4. **Sector / beta neutralization.** Neutralize the score before ranking so the
   top decile is not merely a sector or high-beta tilt.
5. **Wire the legacy comparison.** Capture the existing production prediction
   series over the same window and compute its rank IC head-to-head; the canary
   gate requires this baseline before any `GO_CANDIDATE`.

## Conclusion

`NO_GO` is the correct, honest outcome: the current composite has **no
measurable cross-sectional edge** on this real-data window, the failure is a
signal issue (compounded by a missing legacy baseline) rather than a data or
gate-threshold artifact, and there is no **production edge** of any kind to
promote. The five experiments above are the next things to test before this
model could ever become a canary candidate.
